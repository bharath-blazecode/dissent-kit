# Worked example

## Request

> I want to leave my job and freelance full time next month. I have three months of expenses saved. One client said they are interested, but nothing is signed.

## Direct Review

**Verdict:** Do not resign next month. The main risk is that you are treating interest as revenue while your savings leave little room for a delayed contract or invoice.

**Strongest version of the plan:** You have some runway and a real prospect, so this is not a blind jump.

**Counterargument:** `[certain]` An unsigned prospect owes you nothing. Even if the client signs, payment may arrive weeks after the first work begins.

**What works:** Three months of savings gives you time to test the market. A named prospect is better than starting with no pipeline.

**Missing tradeoff:** Starting part time will slow the transition, but it lets you test pricing, sales, and payment timing before giving up your salary.

**Corrected plan:** Keep the job until you have either one signed contract that covers a defined share of your monthly expenses or a larger cash buffer. Add a second warm lead so one client is not the entire plan. Set a decision trigger instead of a resignation date.

## Single-context deliberation

This example uses one model context. The five lenses broaden the review, but they are not independent evidence.

### Decision frame

Should the user leave a stable job next month with three months of savings, one unsigned prospect, and no stated second lead?

### Pre-mortems

- **Rook, Direct:** The client never signs, and the search for replacement work consumes most of the runway.
- **Bishop, Strategy:** The client signs, but low pricing and slow payment turn three months of savings into a much shorter operating window.
- **Knight, Blind spot:** One client becomes both the buyer and the user's only source of market feedback, which can distort pricing and priorities.
- **Queen, Synthesis:** Contract uncertainty, payment delay, and client concentration compound each other. Solving only one leaves the plan fragile.
- **King, Stakes:** The decision must protect the user's ability to build a freelance business without forcing an early return to unsuitable work.

Across all five lenses, savings are a fact. The client's intent, contract value, payment terms, and wider demand remain unknown.

### Challenge and confidence movement

The initial direct view was to wait only until the first contract was signed, at medium confidence. The strategy and blind-spot lenses exposed client concentration and payment delay. The revised view is to wait for one signed contract plus a second warm lead, at high confidence.

### Verdict

The unresolved issue is how much risk the user can personally absorb. The evidence still does not support resigning next month.

- **Recommendation:** Wait for one signed contract and a second warm lead, or extend the cash buffer.
- **Falsifier:** A signed contract with adequate value, workable payment terms, and evidence of another active prospect would overturn this recommendation.
- **Review point:** Revisit when the contract is signed or 30 days before the planned resignation date, whichever comes first.
- **First action:** Ask the interested client for a paid pilot, written scope, price, and payment schedule.
